# ECI Job Orchestrator

This module defines Job-level coordination within one ECI lifecycle. It changes logical ownership only; it does not change Worker runtime authority, hooks, or planner authority.

## Counts and ownership

- **N** counts admitted outcomes until acceptance or completed cancellation closure, including queued, waiting, blocked, post-Fast, and cancellation-cleanup outcomes. Reopening an accepted outcome counts it again. N governs lifecycle and acceptance only; it never publishes or retires Orchestrators.
- Give each independently requested root work package one stable **Job** identity. Outcomes introduced by its decomposition or extension inherit that Job. Additive follow-ups, repairs, iterations, main/Fast paths, Helpers, owner transfers, and shared targets retain their outcome’s Job. Each outcome has one home Job; reopening restores it.
- **J** counts the distinct home Jobs represented by outcomes still counted in N. A Job enters or leaves J when its first or last home outcome enters or leaves N.
- At **J ≤ 1**, the Supervisor directly coordinates every active outcome. At **J > 1**, publish exactly one Orchestrator for each active Job. That Orchestrator coordinates all outcomes in its Job directly through their Workers. Do not add a separate outcome owner beneath it.
- A new outcome within an active Job changes that Job owner’s outcome set, not the number of Orchestrators. N transitions alone never create or retire an Orchestrator.

| Active work | Logical ownership |
| --- | --- |
| Job A contains A1/A2; J = 1, N = 2 | The Supervisor coordinates both outcomes directly; there is no Orchestrator. |
| Job A contains A1/A2; Job B contains B1; J = 2, N = 3 | Exactly two Orchestrators: A coordinates A1/A2; B coordinates B1. |
| B1 closes, leaving A1/A2; J returns to 1 while N = 2 | A’s Orchestrator hands all of Job A back to the Supervisor. |

At N = 0, the Supervisor performs ordinary lifecycle teardown only after each outcome is accepted or cancellation-closed and all owned Writers are observed stopped.

## Authority and reporting

- The Supervisor owns outcome admission and closure, N/J tracking, global capacity, protected controls, cross-Job visibility, final impact/treatment/dispositions, independent verification of Producer commits, acceptance, and lifecycle teardown.
- An Orchestrator owns routine dispatch, continuation, sequencing, dependencies, evidence reconciliation, progress, and closure preparation for its published Job. It coordinates every home outcome directly and loads the task-local review policy for review dispatch. It cannot admit requested outcomes, publish another owner, or accept work.
- Preserve each outcome’s requirements, bounded assignments, Producer identities, main/Fast paths, independent reviews, post-Fast sequence, required E2E, and acceptance evidence. One Job owner never combines outcomes or their acceptance.
- Name each assignment’s Job, outcome, Supervisor runtime identity, and current published Job-owner runtime identity. Workers send task-local reports directly to the current owner: `followup_task` to an Orchestrator, or `send_message` to the Supervisor when it owns the Job. Send Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor. Automatic runtime-parent delivery does not replace explicit reporting.
- A Producer sends its committed handoff through the Job owner to the Supervisor. The Supervisor verifies the exact immutable commit range before dependent review or work. It returns the verified range and outcome mapping to every affected Job owner. Continue outcome-local work whose dependencies are already satisfied.
- Job coordination does not grant authority to assign disputed content or schedule Git turns. The Supervisor and Orchestrators report identity and scope facts; the actual authors decide their contribution boundaries and commit order directly.

## Publication, transfer, and report drain

- **Initial admission:** one Job means J = 1 and direct Supervisor coordination. For multiple Jobs admitted together, prepare one Orchestrator candidate per Job from the complete admitted set; never collapse Jobs or publish per-outcome owners.
- **J rises above one:** prepare one Orchestrator for each active Job without one. The Supervisor remains that Job’s explicit interim owner until handoff acceptance and publication. Each transfer covers every home outcome. If capacity is unavailable, keep the named interim owner and queue that Job’s candidate.
- **Another Job is admitted while J > 1:** prepare and publish its Orchestrator. Adding or closing outcomes in existing Jobs changes their owners’ assignments, not the number of Orchestrators.
- **Before publication:** the outgoing owner stops new dispatch only for the transferring Job, finishes or accounts for its active coordination call, and transfers requirements, scopes, assignments, evidence, outstanding reports, and current state for every home outcome. Hold affected Job-local reports during transfer; route Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor. Existing Workers continue bounded work.
- **Publication:** the incoming candidate accepts the bounded handoff. The Supervisor rechecks J, Job membership, and current ownership, publishes the single effective owner for that Job, and sends the route to the incoming and outgoing owners and existing Workers. Transfer held reports through the Supervisor. If J or the scope changed, withdraw or recompute; preparation alone grants no dispatch authority.
- **J falls from two to one:** the remaining Orchestrator stops new dispatch and transfers all Job outcomes and reports to the Supervisor, even when N > 1. The old route stays effective during handback, subject to its report hold. Direct Supervisor coordination starts on publication; the outgoing Orchestrator then relays delayed reports only.
- **Retirement and delayed reports:** an owner retires from dispatch when its last home outcome leaves N or its handback is published. Keep its relay turn active until already-produced reports are accounted for. Former, withdrawn, and non-current owners relay reports to the Supervisor without dispatch. The Supervisor decides whether closed-outcome evidence reopens work and forwards an unprocessed active-outcome report to its current owner, or processes it directly when it owns the Job. Do not duplicate already-routed reports or issued reviews.
- **Migration:** transfer any existing per-outcome owner through this protocol. At J > 1, consolidate all home outcomes under one published Job Orchestrator; at J ≤ 1, transfer all outcome coordination to the Supervisor. Retire superseded owners to report relay only. Do not retain an extra hierarchy level or silently expand authority.
- Ownership transfer preserves Producer and reviewer identities, assignments, and runtime ancestry. It does not restart Workers, wait for their acknowledgements, or stop Writers solely because ownership changed. At N = 0, the Supervisor tears down only after closure and Writer shutdown are complete.

Workers explicitly deliver progress and final reports before returning. Preserve the Job, outcome, assignment, original source, report, and evidence references. Use `followup_task` for an Orchestrator and `send_message` for the Supervisor. A published owner processes reports within its assignment except during a handoff hold. A non-current owner relays reports to the Supervisor without dispatch, implementation, review, disposition, acceptance, or lifecycle action. The Supervisor forwards only reports that still need current-owner processing; already-routed evidence needs no duplicate followup. A timeout alone does not trigger a drain.

## Active-writer rows and shared-index coordination

These rules apply to every actor authorized to stage, otherwise mutate the Git index, or commit, including an author making an administrative checkpoint. Participation adds no edit authority. The Supervisor follows the same rules for its own administrative contribution.

1. Each Producer reports only its own active-writer row to its current published Job owner. Other authorized index actors report their rows through their current owner, or directly to the Supervisor when no Job owner applies. Identify the exact runtime identity, contribution scope, home Job and outcome where applicable, repository/worktree, declared write paths or regions, actual Git index identity, current branch, live pending-intent state, and report-owner identity. For every write-capable Helper, pair its exact runtime identity with its write scope in the responsible Producer’s row. Preserve that pairing and Producer identity in returned peer data. An authorized Helper index operation names the actual actor. Use assignments and reports, not a separate registry.
2. Rows match when write scopes may intersect or actors have live pending intents on the same actual Git index. The published Job owner matches rows across its home outcomes and reports them through ordinary coordination to the Supervisor. The Supervisor matches across Jobs and with administrative authors, returning only matching peer rows through published Job owners or directly to actors without one. At J ≤ 1, the Supervisor does this directly. Same-index matching ignores path, Job, outcome, and branch differences.
3. Update a row when actor identity, declared scope, Helper write scope, index, pending intent, or published report owner changes. Route updates to current and previously matched peers to clear superseded facts. Preserve publication and delayed-report relay rules.
4. When declared write scopes may intersect, the Job owner or Supervisor routes matching responsible Producer rows as candidates. Those Producers compare intended and actual changes directly; split/combine and content-ownership agreements apply only when actual contributions overlap or depend on one another. A Helper may provide factual scope or evidence through its responsible Producer, but Helpers and other non-Producer Workers never negotiate split/combine, content ownership, or commit order. Producers decide implementation content; no one waits for user direction.
5. Before its first staging, other index mutation, or commit for a contribution, every authorized actor publishes a live pending intent through the route above and requests a fresh same-index lookup. The Supervisor processes intent installation and lookup of all live intents on that actual index as one serialized information action, completing it before the next install-and-lookup. It returns every matching actor row through the same routes. A queued publication, cached roster, or earlier lookup is not a completed lookup. For its own administrative contribution, the Supervisor installs its intent and performs the lookup in that serialized sequence.

   After the completed same-index lookup, the responsible Producer for each implementation contribution contacts every returned matching Producer directly and agrees index-mutation and commit order for its contribution before any associated Worker or Helper proceeds. If a matching administrative author has its own pending contribution, that author contacts the responsible Producers directly only about its contribution, as specified in rule 6. With three or more contenders, the responsible Producers establish a consistent order across their contributions. Only one actor mutates the shared index at a time. Keep each intent live through staging, the complete staged-result check, and commit, or until safely withdrawn or handed off by direct agreement. A later responsible Producer contacts every Producer returned by its same-index lookup and agrees before associated index mutation. A new intent or changed actual index requires a fresh lookup; an earlier agreement covers only the intent discussed.

   If Producer A’s lookup is processed before Producer B’s, A’s result cannot include B’s later intent. B’s lookup returns A while A’s intent is live, even if responses remain undelivered. B contacts A before staging; the responsible Producers agree the order and any safe handoff of an in-progress operation for their contributions. A follows its completed lookup and any resulting agreement. Lookup processing order grants no commit priority. The Supervisor supplies identity, scope, and index facts; the responsible Producers choose the order. Actual Git index identity determines contention; branch identity supplies context.
6. Responsible Producers—and an administrative author only regarding its own named contribution—contact matching peers by exact runtime identity: `send_message` while running; `followup_task` if idle. In coordination roles, the Supervisor and Orchestrators relay identity/scope facts only. An administrative author, including the Supervisor when it has a named contribution, contacts responsible Producers only to agree the boundary or order of that contribution; it has no authority to assign contested content, arbitrate another author’s contribution, or schedule Git turns.

## Producer handoff commits and joint checkpoints

- When contributions overlap or depend on one another, the involved Producers compare the actual changes and agree whether to split or combine them. There is no automatic Job or lane priority across outcomes. Within an outcome, existing main-path-over-Fast precedence applies to overlap.
- Producers agree content and staging/commit order directly. A named Implementer and Fast Owner are Producers for their bounded contributions. Each commits its own checked scope during handoff and reports the commit ID, immutable parent-to-commit range, scope, exclusions, focused checks, and limitations.
- Immediately before commit, the named Producer compares the complete staged diff and resulting index tree with the intended parent and exact checked, agreed contribution scope, including all joint contributions and exclusions. Commit only that result. Apply the same full staged-result check to administrative commits. If staged content includes another author’s scope or cannot be isolated, preserve all other staged and unstaged work; agree the boundary and order directly with those exact authors, then reinspect the full staged result. Never silently include or clear another author’s work.
- After handoff, the Supervisor independently verifies the exact immutable range before dependent review or work. A Producer commit is a provisional checkpoint, not acceptance. Make repairs in a separate commit; do not amend or erase the earlier checkpoint.
- If changes cannot be separated cleanly, every contributing authorized author agrees the exact bounded joint scope and selects one involved Producer to commit it. This includes administrative authors, including the Supervisor when it authored content. Each author supplies and authorizes its own contribution; all agree on the combined boundary and committing Producer. Helpers provide changes and evidence through their responsible Producer and never commit. Include only completed, checked contributions; exclude unrelated and in-flight work.
- The joint handoff names every contributing author and responsible Producer. Map each exact contribution to its agreed changes, scope, Job/outcome where applicable, focused-check evidence, limitations, and exclusions. Identify administrative contributions, every affected outcome, and the single immutable parent-to-commit range. The named Producer performs the full staged-result check and commits only that scope. The Supervisor verifies the full range, then sends that range and contribution map to each affected Job owner.
- Every affected outcome retains its usual review and acceptance requirements. Review interacting contributions together and attach integrated evidence to every affected outcome. Its Job owner accounts for criteria and evidence; the Supervisor accepts. No author independently reviews its own contribution. Fresh noncontributing reviewers cover Supervisor-authored content. A joint checkpoint does not merge outcomes or count as acceptance.
- A joint checkpoint is the narrow exception to Fast’s separate checkpoint per changed issue when contributions are inseparable. Preserve evidence for each issue; other Fast issues keep separate checkpoints. Repairs remain separate commits.

## Worker and Helper boundaries

- Any Worker, including a Helper, may recursively delegate bounded support within its inherited assignment, authorization, read/write limits, and the Supervisor’s global capacity decisions. Delegation never enlarges authority.
- Every Helper reports through its spawning Worker. Each parent accounts for descendants’ scope, writes, evidence, results, and shutdown. The Job owner accounts for delegated Writers before outcome closure. Helpers add no Job or outcome and do not create a lane by themselves.
- A reviewer personally establishes its independent baseline, verifies evidence, supplies its required verdict, and performs its assigned E2E. Helpers at any depth cannot replace those duties or carry conclusions across independent reviews. Reviewers may delegate bounded support; the current Job owner dispatches required workflow reviews and E2E assignments for each outcome.
- Implementation authority must exist through the full delegation chain. Helpers return changes through their parents to the named Implementer or Fast Owner. That Producer integrates, checks, and commits the bounded contribution under the rules above.
