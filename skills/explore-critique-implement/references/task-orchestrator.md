# ECI Job Orchestrator

This module defines Job-level coordination within one ECI lifecycle. It changes logical ownership only; it does not change Worker runtime authority, hooks, or planner authority.

## Counts and ownership

- **N** counts admitted outcomes until acceptance or completed cancellation closure, including queued, waiting, blocked, post-Fast, and cancellation-cleanup outcomes. Reopening an accepted outcome counts it again. N governs lifecycle and acceptance only; it never publishes or retires Orchestrators.
- Give each independently requested root work package one stable **Job** identity. Outcomes introduced by its decomposition or extension inherit that Job. Additive follow-ups, repairs, iterations, main/Fast paths, Helpers, owner transfers, and shared targets retain their outcome’s Job. Each outcome has one home Job; reopening restores it.
- **J** counts the distinct home Jobs represented by outcomes still counted in N. A Job enters or leaves J when its first or last home outcome enters or leaves N.
- At **J ≤ 1**, the Supervisor directly coordinates every active outcome. At **J > 1**, publish exactly one Orchestrator for each active Job. That Orchestrator coordinates all outcomes in its Job directly through their Workers. Do not add a separate outcome owner beneath it.
- A new outcome within an active Job changes that Job owner’s outcome set, not the number of Orchestrators. N transitions alone never create or retire an Orchestrator.

| Active work | Logical ownership |
| --- | --- |
| Job A contains A1/A2; J = 1, N = 2 | The Supervisor coordinates both outcomes directly; there is no Orchestrator. |
| Job A contains A1/A2; Job B contains B1; J = 2, N = 3 | Exactly two Orchestrators: A coordinates A1/A2; B coordinates B1. |
| B1 closes, leaving A1/A2; J returns to 1 while N = 2 | A’s Orchestrator hands all of Job A back to the Supervisor. |

At N = 0, the Supervisor performs ordinary lifecycle teardown only after each outcome is accepted or cancellation-closed and all owned Writers are observed stopped.

## Authority and reporting

- The Supervisor owns outcome admission and closure, N/J tracking, global capacity, protected controls, cross-Job visibility, final impact/treatment/dispositions, independent verification of Producer commits, acceptance, and lifecycle teardown.
- An Orchestrator owns routine dispatch, continuation, sequencing, dependencies, evidence reconciliation, progress, and closure preparation for its published Job. It coordinates every home outcome directly and loads the task-local review policy for review dispatch. It cannot admit requested outcomes, publish another owner, or accept work.
- Preserve each outcome’s requirements, bounded assignments, Producer identities, main/Fast paths, independent reviews, post-Fast sequence, required E2E, and acceptance evidence. One Job owner never combines outcomes or their acceptance.
- Name each assignment’s Job, outcome, Supervisor runtime identity, and current published Job-owner runtime identity. Workers send task-local reports directly to the current owner: `followup_task` to an Orchestrator, or `send_message` to the Supervisor when it owns the Job. Send Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor. Automatic runtime-parent delivery does not replace explicit reporting.
- A Producer sends its committed handoff through the Job owner to the Supervisor. The Supervisor verifies the exact immutable commit range before dependent review or work. It returns the verified range and outcome mapping to every affected Job owner. Continue outcome-local work whose dependencies are already satisfied.
- Job coordination does not grant authority to assign disputed content or schedule Git turns. The Supervisor and Orchestrators report identity, scope, and index facts. Responsible Producers decide implementation boundaries and commit order directly; Helpers report through their parents and follow their responsible Producer’s agreement.

## Publication, transfer, and report drain

- **Initial admission:** one Job means J = 1 and direct Supervisor coordination. For multiple Jobs admitted together, prepare one Orchestrator candidate per Job from the complete admitted set; never collapse Jobs or publish per-outcome owners.
- **J rises above one:** prepare one Orchestrator for each active Job without one. The Supervisor remains that Job’s explicit interim owner until handoff acceptance and publication. Each transfer covers every home outcome. If capacity is unavailable, keep the named interim owner and queue that Job’s candidate.
- **Another Job is admitted while J > 1:** prepare and publish its Orchestrator. Adding or closing outcomes in existing Jobs changes their owners’ assignments, not the number of Orchestrators.
- **Before publication:** the outgoing owner stops new dispatch only for the transferring Job, finishes or accounts for its active coordination call, and transfers requirements, scopes, assignments, evidence, outstanding reports, and current state for every home outcome. Hold affected Job-local reports during transfer; route Supervisor-owned decisions to the Supervisor. Existing Workers continue bounded work.
- **Publication:** the incoming candidate accepts the bounded handoff. The Supervisor rechecks J, Job membership, and current ownership, publishes the single effective owner for that Job, and sends the route to the incoming and outgoing owners and existing Workers. Transfer held reports through the Supervisor. If J or the scope changed, withdraw or recompute; preparation alone grants no dispatch authority.
- **J falls from two to one:** the remaining Orchestrator stops new dispatch and transfers all Job outcomes and reports to the Supervisor, even when N > 1. The old route stays effective during handback, subject to its report hold. Direct Supervisor coordination starts on publication; the outgoing Orchestrator then relays delayed reports only.
- **Retirement and delayed reports:** an owner retires from dispatch when its last home outcome leaves N or its handback is published. Keep its relay turn active until already-produced reports are accounted for. Former, withdrawn, and non-current owners relay reports to the Supervisor without dispatch. The Supervisor decides whether closed-outcome evidence reopens work and forwards an unprocessed active-outcome report to its current owner, or processes it directly when it owns the Job. Do not duplicate already-routed reports or issued reviews.
- **Migration:** transfer any existing per-outcome owner through this protocol. At J > 1, consolidate all home outcomes under one published Job Orchestrator; at J ≤ 1, transfer all outcome coordination to the Supervisor. Retire superseded owners to report relay only. Do not retain an extra hierarchy level or silently expand authority.
- Ownership transfer preserves Producer and reviewer identities, assignments, and runtime ancestry. It does not restart Workers, wait for their acknowledgements, or stop Writers solely because ownership changed. At N = 0, the Supervisor tears down only after closure and Writer shutdown are complete.

Workers explicitly deliver progress and final reports before returning. Preserve the Job, outcome, assignment, original source, report, and evidence references. Use `followup_task` for an Orchestrator and `send_message` for the Supervisor. A published owner processes reports within its assignment except during a handoff hold. A non-current owner relays reports to the Supervisor without dispatch, implementation, review, disposition, acceptance, or lifecycle action. The Supervisor forwards only reports that still need current-owner processing; already-routed evidence needs no duplicate followup. A timeout alone does not trigger a drain.

## Active Job compliance

The current Job owner immediately critiques significant-movement and final Worker reports against actual work, original requirements, and applicable ECI duties. Check role authority, skills, model selection, delegation, and completion evidence; a positive report or unchanged assignment is insufficient. Resolve concrete deviations through the responsible Worker and verify its correction. Continue unaffected work.

Retain the last roster-review time in existing coordination state. About every half-hour while the Job actively executes, refresh its active roster at the next normal coordination cycle: actor, runtime parent, assigned role/profile, and actual current work, including Helpers. Bound an active coordination wait by the next due review, roughly 1800000 ms at most; idle Jobs retain ordinary waits. The current owner accounts for descendants. Explicitly confirm or critique each actor's ECI compliance from current assignments, reports, and relevant evidence; retain the verdict, correction, or unknown and next check. Send the roster and findings to the Supervisor in the ordinary Job update. Update changed entries when significant movement is processed and carry the last check through owner handoff. Do one current review when due; add no catch-up bursts, idle wakeups, background polling, separate registry format, or checker agents.

## Active-writer rows and shared-index coordination

These rules apply to every actor authorized to stage or otherwise mutate the Git index. Only Producers commit. Assign every new tracked repository contribution—including documentation, configuration, analysis, and administrative deliverables—to a Producer to author, check, and commit. Supervisors and Orchestrators author no new tracked contribution, stage no repository change, and commit nothing. Preserve any pre-existing authorized Supervisor-authored content only through an explicit bounded handoff that records its original author, scope, and provenance. Participation adds no edit authority; Helpers follow their responsible Producer’s agreement and never commit.

Use the [typed Worker Git routes](../../../hooks/gate-escape-hatches.md) for assigned paths: `$HOME/.codex/bin/eci-worker-git --repo <repository>` provides fixed staging, unstage, restore, remove, move, and prepared-index commit operations with literal paths and protected-target checks. Native Worker mutations in the catalog's routed families are referred to this CLI; other native Git operations retain the catalog's defaults. Unstage exact owned paths with `unstage -- <paths>` and correct committed content in a new checkpoint. Amendments, history rewrites, and arbitrary Git argument passthrough remain unsupported by typed operations; the catalog documents a `run-once` exception requiring explicit user authorization for the exact command and environment, a nonblank reason, and acknowledgment. The CLI cannot verify Producer assignment, hunk ownership, or completed coordination; the rules below still apply outside that exact authorized exception.

1. Each Producer reports only its own active-writer row to its current published Job owner. Other authorized index actors, including Helpers, report through their current owner or responsible Producer. Each row identifies the exact runtime identity, contribution scope, responsible Producer, home Job and outcome where applicable, repository/worktree, declared write paths or regions, actual Git index identity, current branch, live pending-intent state, and report-owner identity. A Producer row names any pre-existing Supervisor-authored scope explicitly handed to it. For every write-capable Helper descendant, pair its exact runtime identity with its write scope in the responsible Producer’s row. Preserve those pairings and the responsible Producer’s identity in returned matching peer data. An authorized Helper index operation names the actual actor. Use existing assignments and reports, not a separate registry.
2. Rows match when declared write scopes may intersect or actors have live pending intents on the same actual Git index. The published Job owner matches rows across all its home outcomes and reports them through ordinary coordination to the Supervisor. The Supervisor matches across Jobs and returns only matching peer rows through published Job owners or directly to Producers without one. At J ≤ 1, the Supervisor performs these owner duties directly. Same-index matching ignores path, Job, outcome, and branch differences, including different outcomes in the same Job.
3. Update a row when actor identity, declared scope, Helper write scope, index, pending intent, or published report owner changes. Route updates to current and previously matched peers to clear superseded facts. Preserve publication and delayed-report relay rules.
4. When declared write scopes may intersect, the Job owner or Supervisor routes each matching row’s exact actor identity, responsible Producer identity, and the scope intersection or actual Git index identity that caused the match to every affected responsible Producer. Only the responsible Producers compare intended and actual changes and decide whether to split or combine contributions that overlap or depend on one another. Helpers report intended and actual changes through their parents to their responsible Producers; those Producers integrate and check Helper work. Helpers do not contact peers or negotiate. Producers resolve implementation boundaries without waiting for user direction.
5. Before its first staging, other index mutation, or commit for a contribution, every authorized index actor publishes a live pending intent through the route above and requests a fresh same-index lookup. The Supervisor processes intent installation and lookup of all live intents on that actual Git index as one serialized information action, completing it before processing the next install-and-lookup for that index. It returns every matching actor row, including responsible Producer identity, through the same routes. A queued publication, cached roster, or earlier lookup is not a completed lookup.

   After the completed lookup, only responsible Producers for returned matching rows contact one another directly and agree index-mutation and commit order for their contributions before any associated Worker proceeds. If a Helper or other non-Producer has the pending intent, its responsible Producer participates on its behalf; the actor reports through its parent and follows the Producer agreement without contacting peers or negotiating. With three or more Producer contenders, their agreements establish a consistent order. Only one actor mutates the shared index at a time. Keep each intent live through staging, the complete staged-result check, and commit, or until safely withdrawn or handed off by direct Producer agreement. A later Producer contacts only Producers returned as matching by its same-index lookup and agrees with them before any index mutation. A new intent or changed actual index requires a new lookup; an earlier agreement covers only the intent discussed.

   If Producer A’s lookup is processed before Producer B’s, A’s result cannot include B’s later intent. B’s lookup returns A while A’s intent is live, even if neither response has been delivered. B contacts A before staging; the Producers agree the order and any safe handoff of an in-progress operation. A follows its completed lookup and any resulting agreement. Lookup processing order grants no commit priority. The Supervisor supplies identity, scope, and index facts; the involved Producers choose the order. Actual Git index identity determines contention; branch identity supplies context.
6. Only responsible Producers of matching rows contact one another directly by exact runtime identity: `send_message` while running; `followup_task` if idle. Helpers and other non-Producer Workers report through their parent and follow their responsible Producer’s agreement; they do not contact peers or negotiate. The Supervisor and Orchestrators relay matching identity, scope, and index facts only; they do not assign content or schedule Git turns.

## Producer handoff commits and joint checkpoints

- When contributions overlap or depend on one another, the involved Producers compare the actual changes and agree whether to split or combine them. There is no automatic Job or lane priority across outcomes. Within an outcome, existing main-path-over-Fast precedence applies to overlap.
- Producers agree content boundaries and staging/commit order directly. A named Implementer and Fast Owner are Producers for their bounded contributions. Each commits its checked scope during handoff and reports the commit ID, immutable parent-to-commit range, scope, exclusions, focused checks, and limitations.
- Immediately before commit, the named Producer compares the complete staged diff and resulting index tree with the intended parent and exact checked, agreed contribution scope, including every joint contribution and exclusion. Commit only that result. If pre-existing staged content falls outside the agreed scope or cannot be isolated, preserve other Producers’ staged and unstaged work and coordinate the boundary and order directly with the exact affected Producers. Reinspect the complete staged result after that coordination; never silently include or clear another Producer’s work.
- After handoff, the Supervisor independently verifies the exact immutable range before dependent review or work. A Producer commit is a provisional checkpoint, not acceptance. Make repairs in a separate commit; do not amend or erase the earlier checkpoint.
- If Producer contributions cannot be separated cleanly, every contributing Producer agrees the exact bounded joint scope and selects one involved Producer to commit it. Each supplies and authorizes its own contribution. If the joint scope includes pre-existing authorized Supervisor-authored content, the Supervisor explicitly hands off and approves only that exact content for inclusion; the involved Producers still choose the boundary and committing Producer. Helpers supply their work and evidence through their responsible Producer and never commit. Include only completed, checked contributions; exclude unrelated and in-flight work.
- The joint handoff names every contributing Producer and separately identifies any handed-off pre-existing Supervisor-authored content with its original author, exact scope, and provenance. Map each contribution to its agreed changes, Job/outcome where applicable, focused-check evidence, limitations, and exclusions. List every affected outcome and the single immutable parent-to-commit range. The named Producer commits only that scope after the complete staged-result check. The Supervisor independently verifies the entire range and sends it with the contribution mapping to every affected Job owner.
- Every affected outcome retains its normal review and acceptance requirements. Review interacting contributions together and attach integrated evidence to every affected outcome. Its Job owner accounts for criteria and evidence; the Supervisor accepts. No contributing Producer supplies the required independent review of its own contribution. A joint checkpoint does not merge outcomes or count as acceptance.
- A joint checkpoint is the narrow exception to Fast’s separate checkpoint per changed issue when contributions are inseparable. Preserve evidence for each issue; other Fast issues keep separate checkpoints. Repairs remain separate commits.

## Worker and Helper boundaries

- Any Worker, including a Helper, may recursively delegate bounded support within its inherited assignment, authorization, read/write limits, and the Supervisor’s global capacity decisions. Delegation never enlarges authority.
- Every Helper reports through its spawning Worker. Each parent accounts for descendants’ scope, writes, evidence, results, and shutdown. The Job owner accounts for delegated Writers before outcome closure. Helpers add no Job or outcome and do not create a lane by themselves.
- A reviewer personally establishes its independent baseline, verifies evidence, supplies its required verdict, and performs its assigned E2E. Helpers at any depth cannot replace those duties or carry conclusions across independent reviews. Reviewers may delegate bounded support; the current Job owner dispatches required workflow reviews and E2E assignments for each outcome.
- Implementation authority must exist through the full delegation chain. Helpers return changes through their parents to the named Implementer or Fast Owner. That Producer integrates, checks, and commits the bounded contribution under the rules above.
